Hairmaxxing 101: The Ultimate Guide to Hair Transformation

Hair is often referred to as a man’s makeup. Why? Because unlike your jawline or skin—which demand months of effort—your hair can be transformed in a matter of hours with just a bit of knowledge and intention. Yet, while the internet is flooded with jawline workouts and skincare hacks, hair remains criminally underrated in the world of looksmaxxing.

Most people overlook the power of hair. It frames your face, defines your style, and elevates your presence in a way no other feature can. But because it’s rarely talked about, navigating hair care and styling becomes a trial-and-error game—wasting both time and confidence.

That’s why I’ve created this article.

Inside, you’ll find everything you need to know to transform your hair—from understanding your hair type and texture, to choosing styles that complement your face shape, to simple actionable that actually work. It’s a bigger article than usual, because I’ve condensed all the tested, handpicked knowledge you need in one place.

Just reading this alone will give you the roadmap to hairmaxxing—and actionable steps you can implement immediately to take your appearance to the next level.

1. Hair Basics

• Type:

Before styling or optimizing your hair, it’s necessary to understand what you’re working with. There are 4 primary hair types:

  • 1 - Straight (a: no waves, b: slight waves, c: stronger waves)
  • 2 - Wavy (a: loose waves, b: defined waves, c: wavy with curls)
  • 3 - Curly (a: curls up mildly, b: defined curls, c: stronger curls)>
  • 4 - Coily(a: losely coiled, b: moderate coils, c: untamable coils)

You can determine your hair type by combing down your hair and observing how they behave. You can also let your hair air dry after a shower to see if they curl up. Compare it to a hair type chart to identify yours.

• Texture:

texture is basically how your hair feels in your hand, it determines the volume and shine of your hairstyle. Usually hair is either fine (thin), medium or coarse (thick). Coarse or thick hair usually gives you volume but not shine while fine or thin hair gives you shine and not volume. It is essential to know this to decide your hairstyle, if you have thick hair and then you should focus on volume control on the other hand if you have thin hair you should focus on styling in ways that adds volume to your hair.

• Porosity:

• Porosity: Hair porosity is how well your hair holds moisture. Though it doesn’t play a major role in styling, it is crucial to know your hair porosity to follow a proper hair care routine that prevents your hair from being too greasy or being too dry and frizzy, because no matter how well you style your hair if your hair is dry and frizzy or greasy then it can never look good on you. To find your hair porosity there are two most accurate ways:

  • Water Float Test: 1. The water float test: take a few clean stands of your hair that doesn’t have any product on them and drop then in a glass of water. Wait for 3-5 mins and observe it.
    • Floats at the top = Low porosity (resists moisture)
    • Settles in the middle = Medium porosity(ideal)
    • Sinks at the bottom = High porosity (absorbs moisture quickly but also loses it quickly)
  • Slip ’n’ Slide Test:: hold a clean strand of hair without any products in them in-between your fingers and slide it in upward direction from the tip to the root.
    • If they feel smooth = Low porosity
    • If it feels bumpy or coarse = High porosity

2. Choosing the Right Hairstyle for You

Most men don’t know how to decide the right hairstyle for them and usually keep the hairstyle that they had from childhood or get themselves the most trending hairstyle. In most cases, either way they end up with a hairstyle that doesn’t really complement their face. Finding the perfect hairstyle is determined by many factors:

i. Hair Quality:

While this does not really affect finding your hairstyle, it is necessary that you have the best quality of hair for your hair type, only then you can style it anyway you want.

ii. Face Shape:

face shape plays a crucial role in deciding the right hairstyle for you, but before you know which hairstyle suits your face shape, you must know your face shape. And you don’t need fancy apps or drawings on your mirror to find your face shape, all you need is to follow the steps below:

  1. Stand in front of a mirror and brush back your hair in a way where your entire face, hairline and jawline is visible.
  2. Measure your:
    • Forehead Width – Measure the widest point halfway between eyebrows and hairline
    • Cheekbone Width – Measure from one cheekbone (outermost corners of the eye where you can feel the widest part of your midface) to the other, the tape or ruler should pass across your nose bridge.
    • Jawline – Measure from the tip of your chin to just below your ear on one side and multiply it by two
    • Face Length – Measure from the top of your forehead (hairline) down to your chin.
  3. Compare results:
    • Oval – Face length > cheekbones > forehead > jawline (rounded jaw)
    • Round – Similar measurements in length and width, soft jawline.
    • Square – All measurements are fairly equal, but with a strong, angular jaw
    • Oblong – Face length is the largest, with similar cheekbone, forehead, and jaw widths
    • Heart – Forehead is the widest, cheekbones medium, jawline narrow with pointed chin
    • Diamond – Face length > cheekbones > forehead > jawline (sharp features, narrow forehead and jaw)
    • Triangle – Jawline is the widest, followed by cheekbones and a narrow forehead

iii. Hairstyle:

Once you’ve known your face shape you now know the weakness and strengths of your facial harmony and can manipulate them for your advantage. For example, if you have a diamond shaped face, getting hairstyles like a middle part or the quiff can soften those wide cheekbones and can make up to the narrow forehead. Or if you have a square face you can flex those strong angles and equal harmony by getting cuts like a buzz cut. We have listed below the best hairstyles for each face shape with visuals to help you understand better and choose the right hairstyle for you.

Round Face

  • Best for: Adding height, reducing width
  • Go for: High volume on top, short sides
  • Avoid: Full fringes, rounded cuts, buzz or bowl cuts
  • Best hairstyles: textured quiff, faux hawk, pompadour with midfade, side swept fringe.

Square Face

  • Best for: Emphasizing strong jawlines and equal facial harmony
  • Go for: short clean cuts, classic fades or slick back look
  • Avoid: Flat cuts that add width, boxy styles that exaggerate the squareness
  • Best haircuts: buzz cut, French crop, textured crop, slicked back undercut, mod cuts

Oval Face

  • Best for: Most versatile. Can rock almost any hairstyles
  • Go for: Almost any style fades, side parts, crops, fringes, flows and slick backs
  • Avoid: Styles that cover the forehead completely like bangs or certain fringes as this can ruin the balance and harmony
  • Best hairstyles: side part with low taper fade, the dream flow, mod cuts, surfer curtains

Diamond Face

  • Best for: Widening forehead and jaw while softening the cheekbone
  • Go for: Styles like fringes, quiffs, dream flow, curtains
  • Avoid: Too much volume on top alone
  • Best hairstyles: any curtain hairstyles like surfer curtains, middlepart flow, the textured quiff, shaggy mullet

Oblong / Rectangular

  • Best for: Reducing face length
  • Go for: Medium-length sides, volume on the sides like side swept bangs, tapered flow, curtains flow
  • Avoid: Tall pompadours or tight fades that thin out the sides and add volume on top
  • Best hairstyles: classic side part, surfer curtains, dream flow, middle part

Heart-Shaped

  • Best for: Balancing wider forehead
  • Go for: Side part, medium length with volume near chin
  • Avoid: Styles that exaggerate forehead
  • Best hairstyles: surfer curtains, middle part flow, classic slick back
